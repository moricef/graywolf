Live Map

Live map of stations heard on RF and APRS-IS

Every station you hear — on RF or through APRS-IS — lands on the live map with its APRS symbol and callsign. The map keeps itself current in the background and zooms automatically to cover the stations you’re hearing right now.

Pick a basemap on the Maps page: the Graywolf basemap (free, takes one click to register your station) or plain OpenStreetMap. You can also pre-download maps for the states you operate in, so the map keeps working when you’re off-grid.

The live map with stations, trails, and weather layers enabled

Layer Controls

The layer panel (top-left, below the zoom controls) lets you toggle what’s displayed on the map:

LayerDescription
Stations APRS station markers with standard symbol icons and callsign labels. Click a marker for position, path, comment, and heard-via details.
Trails Polyline trails showing the recent movement path of mobile stations.
Weather Temperature labels and meteorological wind barbs for stations reporting weather.
My Position Your station’s position marker, taken from your GPS or your last beacon.
Fixed Points Operator-defined landmarks stored by Graywolf and shared with every browser connected to it.
Direction Findings Manual radio bearings entered from station popups. Orange rays start at the reporting station’s recorded position.
Direct RX Off by default. When on, the map only shows stations you’ve heard directly on RF inside the time range — digipeated and APRS-IS (iGated) packets are hidden. A station qualifies if at least one of its packets in the window arrived with zero digi hops.
✓

On mobile devices, the layer panel collapses to a button to save screen space. Tap the layers icon to expand it.

Time Range

The time range picker controls how far back the map looks. Stations not heard inside that window drop off the map. Pick from 1 hour, 2 hours, 4 hours, 8 hours, 12 hours, 1 day, 2 days, 4 days, or 7 days. Anything beyond a few hours needs the Position Log enabled, otherwise older stations get cleared on restart.

Station Popups

Click any station marker to see a popup with detailed information:

  • Callsign and APRS symbol
  • Position — latitude, longitude, altitude, and Maidenhead grid square
  • Heard via — RF direct, RF via digipeaters (with hop count), or APRS-IS
  • Digipeater path — the full path the packet traversed
  • Comment — the station’s comment field
  • Last heard — how recently the station was heard
  • Actions — add a direction-finding bearing, send a message, open the APRS logs, or look up the station on QRZ

For a weather station, the popup also includes a compact observation card with a wind-direction compass and every value present in the APRS report: temperature, wind and gust, humidity, pressure, rainfall, snowfall, and solar radiation. Graywolf calculates dew point when temperature and humidity are available, and wind chill when the standard cold-weather formula applies. Rain since midnight is shown as Rain today. Values follow the metric or imperial choice on the Preferences page.

Select Pin in a station popup to detach it from the marker and keep it as a floating station window. Desktop browsers can keep several windows open at once; drag their title bars to arrange them, or use the title-bar controls to minimize, close, or center the map on a station. The values continue to refresh while the window is open. On a phone, pinned windows form a horizontally scrollable row of cards along the bottom of the map.

Manual Direction Findings

When a field operator reports a bearing by voice, click that operator’s station marker and select Add bearing. Enter the true bearing, frequency, observation time, estimated angular uncertainty, and optional mission and note. Bearings are measured clockwise from true north in the direction of the transmitter.

Graywolf stores the reporting station’s coordinates with the observation and draws an orange ray from that position. The ray therefore stays attached to the place where the measurement was made even if the APRS station moves later. Click a ray to review its details or delete an erroneous observation. Use the Direction Findings layer switch to hide or show all rays. The observations are stored in the Graywolf database and are shared across connected browsers.

✓

Give related observations the same mission name. With two or more bearings, Graywolf calculates a weighted probable position and a 95% uncertainty area for each mission. A green point and a transparent area with fine green diagonal hatching and a thin green outline indicate a coherent solution. A red point warns that the bearings are nearly parallel, disagree strongly, or point away from the calculated crossing. Click the point for its calculated WGS84 latitude/longitude, eight-character Maidenhead locator and diagnostics; always treat reflected signals and poor mountain geometry with caution.

Distance Ruler

Select Ruler on the right of the map, then click the start and end points. A blue segment shows the measured path; the readout displays surface distance in metres or kilometres and the true bearing from the first point toward the second. After the first click, moving the pointer previews the measurement. You can measure directly over station markers or direction-finding estimates. Select Clear or press Escape to remove it, or select Ruler again to start another measurement. Measurements are temporary and are not transmitted or stored.

Status Bar

The status bar across the bottom of the map shows:

  • Position readout — latitude, longitude, and Maidenhead grid square under the pointer (desktop) or at the center of the map (phone/tablet), with the current zoom level.
  • live — lit when the map is keeping itself zoomed on the stations you’re hearing. Pan or zoom yourself and it switches off; click live to turn it back on.
  • Station count — how many stations are currently on the map.
  • Time range — the time window you’ve picked.
  • Last fetch — seconds since the map last refreshed.
